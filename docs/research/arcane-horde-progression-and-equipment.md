# Arcane Horde Progression and Equipment Proposal

## Proposal

The organized arcane force should be a horde of goblins, orcs, and trolls led
by a very small caster caste. Casters are not merely fragile support units: they
are the army's senior leaders, magical specialists, anchor authorities, and
ritual participants. Mundane captains interpret their intent locally, while the
non-caster majority supplies scouting, formation fighting, shock action,
engineering, labor, and physical endurance.

This document explores progression, roles, perks, and equipment consistent with
the canonical arcane rules. It is a proposal, not accepted content. Counts,
named outcomes, species roles, equipment, and progression depth remain open
until separately accepted.

The later canonical decision establishes that every senior caster normally
leads two or three persistent magical assistants. The proposal below therefore
uses **caster** to mean senior caster unless stated otherwise. Assistant spell
content and progression remain proposed, but their existence, normal cell size,
lesser magical capability, and ritual-support role are settled in
[Arcane Civilization Forces](../arcane-forces.md).

## Force hierarchy

A representative arcane force might contain only three to six senior casters
among scores of non-casters:

```text
senior caster circle
        ↓ intent, magic, rituals, and anchors
caster cells with 2–3 magical assistants
        ↓ lesser magic and ritual support
orc captains and goblin bosses
        ↓ local interpretation and execution
goblin screens, orc formations, and trolls
```

One senior caster is the commander and the others lead major warbands, maintain
anchors, perform individual magic, and contribute to rituals. Each normally has
two or three persistent magical assistants. A senior caster should not be
required for every small formation. Orc captains, goblin bosses, troll handlers,
and standard bearers execute prior intent and make bounded local decisions.

This is organization rather than mind control. Losing a caster removes magical
capability, strategic replanning, and access to some anchored coordination; it
does not make every subordinate passive. Conversely, mundane officers cannot
cast, modify rituals, or fully replace a caster's authority.

Rituals create a specific command risk. A minor working may use one complete
caster cell, but assembling a major quorum means gathering several of the
army's scarce senior leaders and their assistants at one exposed site and
temporarily removing their ordinary spells and battlefield attention. A major
ritual is therefore also a concentration and interruption of command.

## Caster identity

Each caster combines three identities:

- a political and command office;
- a personal spell repertoire; and
- an approach to anchors, rituals, perception, and magical risk.

Broad development families fit this combination better than rigid caster
subclasses. Every caster retains the baseline capabilities promised by the
faction; progression changes how those capabilities create decisions.

### Authority outcomes

- **Imprinted Intent** leaves a formation with a more elaborate standing plan
  before it departs anchored influence.
- **Delegated Voice** permits a designated non-caster captain to make limited
  changes within that intent.
- **Many as One** prepares a coordinated group action among several subordinate
  formations.
- **Last Injunction** establishes a final contingency before the caster
  withdraws or becomes incapacitated. It may also apply to a future recovery
  action if one is accepted.
- **Cruel Priority** declares which formations an overloaded anchor abandons
  first, making load shedding controlled but politically and tactically costly.

### Anchoring outcomes

- **Standard Bearer** establishes a weaker mobile anchor through a bound
  standard that must be physically carried and defended.
- **Twin Allegiance** transfers a formation between overlapping anchors with
  less disruption.
- **Deep Foundation** prepares a stronger stationary anchor at greater cost and
  with a larger magical signature.
- **Burden Reader** identifies which formations or effects are driving
  instability rather than returning only a generic warning.
- **Ordered Collapse** converts imminent overload into a deliberate shutdown,
  sacrificing coordination and potentially the anchor to reduce catastrophe.

These outcomes change capacity management rather than applying unconditional
percentage increases to anchor capacity.

### Strain and casting outcomes

- **Quiet Recovery** is a conditional proposal: if the final Strain rule permits
  recovery, it trades rate for a weaker magical signature.
- **Violent Recovery** is the corresponding fast, conspicuous proposal and may
  disturb nearby units or terrain.
- **Blood Geometry** allows HP empowerment to alter a different spell aspect,
  such as shape rather than intensity.
- **Scarred Channel** changes a caster's future breach possibilities after
  surviving a serious breach without removing the danger.
- **Last Reserve** permits an exceptionally dangerous cast while badly strained
  and makes it a genuine final measure rather than an efficient rotation.

### Ritual outcomes

- **Ritual Conductor** makes a circle less vulnerable to minor disruption
  without replacing the required caster quorum.
- **Redundant Circle** allows one prepared additional participant to replace a
  broken quorum member.
- **Patient Omen** trades longer, more visible preparation for a more
  controllable culmination area.
- **Bound Trigger** expands the observable conditions to which a prepared
  ritual trap may respond.
- **Gatewright** changes portal preparation, wave control, or closure without
  creating personnel.
- **Profane Cartographer** makes better use of legitimate scouting reports when
  selecting geographical targets without tracking hidden units.

### Perception outcomes

- **Borrowed Eyes** permits attunement to a broader category of harmless
  critters.
- **Familiar Territory** improves recognition that an attuned animal's route or
  behavior has been manipulated.
- **Vital Reading** distinguishes broad vitality categories through obstruction,
  such as one large presence from several small ones.
- **Lingering Impression** retains a coarse indication that something recently
  left a sensed location.
- **Watcher's Patience** sustains scrying or attunement with a different
  strain-versus-signature tradeoff.

These outcomes must not turn vague arcane sensing into human-quality
identification or expose authoritative facts that no observer acquired.

## The non-caster horde

The horde is not disposable filler. It allows the faction to remain functional
while casters accumulate Strain, recover under any future rule, conduct
rituals, or die. Species
should create different tactical bodies and forms of cooperation rather than a
linear light-medium-heavy upgrade sequence.

### Goblins: dispersion and opportunism

Organized arcane goblins are numerous, clever, lightly equipped, and
individually vulnerable. They may serve as:

- scouts, infiltrators, archers, slingers, and skirmishers;
- sappers, trap layers, and ritual attendants;
- critter handlers, component carriers, and crews; and
- dispersed groups able to exploit gaps without continuous supervision.

Candidate outcomes include:

- **Gutter Route** traverses constrained passages unavailable to larger units.
- **Scatter** disperses efficiently from area fire at a cost to cohesion.
- **Pack Ambush** prepares a cooperative close attack by several goblins.
- **Trapwise** identifies and handles prepared hazards more safely.
- **Snatch and Carry** rapidly recovers light components, devices, or
  unattended equipment.
- **Vicious Independence** produces opportunistic local behavior rather than
  passivity after separation from anchored direction.

These are citizens and soldiers of the organized civilization. They remain
distinct from the unaffiliated goblins released by a goblin portal.

### Orcs: formation and disciplined violence

Orcs are the dependable military core rather than generic high-HP melee units.
They may provide shielded line infantry, spear or polearm formations, assault
troops, archers, caster bodyguards, warband captains, and anchor defenders.

Candidate outcomes include:

- **Shield Kin** lets adjacent cooperating shields create facing-dependent
  protection.
- **Press the Line** advances slowly while preserving formation and readiness.
- **Receive the Charge** prepares a formation response against an enemy crossing
  its front.
- **Caster's Guard** coordinates movement and reactions around a protected
  caster rather than granting a passive defence aura.
- **Blooded Autonomy** executes standing intent more competently after anchor
  loss.
- **Hold the Standard** preserves local order long enough to recover a fallen or
  displaced anchor bearer.

Orcs are the part of the force that takes and holds the geography required by
anchoring.

### Trolls: living heavy assets

Trolls sit between heavy infantry, engineering vehicles, and monsters. They may
provide frontal assault, mobile cover, obstacle destruction, heavy transport,
casualty recovery, and short-ranged siege attacks.

Candidate outcomes include:

- **Living Rampart** permits smaller allies to use the troll deliberately as
  moving cover, exposing it to concentrated fire.
- **Siege Hands** attacks barriers and deployables efficiently.
- **Root and Mend** trades movement and action for more effective regeneration.
- **Carry the Circle** transports unusually heavy ritual or anchor components
  and becomes a priority target.
- **Sweeping Advance** displaces several smaller units across a broad close arc
  instead of concentrating damage on one.
- **Protective Fury** reacts when a nearby bonded caster or handler is attacked,
  potentially abandoning the troll's previous task.

Likely counters include anti-armour weapons, concentrated precision, effects
that suppress regeneration, constrained terrain, and exploitation of their
large footprint.

## Arcane equipment

Arcane equipment should be less modular than human industrial equipment. It
expresses species, social office, craft tradition, and prepared magical
infrastructure. Items trade weight, preparation, signature, components, anchor
load, and vulnerability rather than forming successively stronger tiers.

### Caster equipment

- staff, wand, blade, or another casting focus;
- grimoire, tablets, knots, or inscribed memory aids;
- component satchel;
- ward tokens and boundary stakes;
- scrying vessel;
- ritual-circle kit;
- prepared portal frame;
- bound standard or anchor stone;
- ceremonial armour; and
- healing or strain-management implements.

A focus might alter which spell aspects accept empowerment. A ritual kit might
change preparation burden. A bound standard might provide mobility at lower
capacity than a fixed anchor. No item should eliminate strain, breach risk,
ritual quorum, or legitimate observation requirements.

### Goblin equipment

- shortbows, slings, knives, and light spears;
- nets, hooks, climbing gear, and ropes;
- smoke, irritant, fire, or obscuring pots;
- traps and local alarms;
- critter cages and feed;
- component panniers; and
- scavenged human equipment used with appropriate limitations.

### Orc equipment

- shields, spears, polearms, axes, cleavers, and heavy bows;
- lamellar, mail, hides, and reinforced helmets;
- pavises and portable barriers;
- breaching tools;
- standards; and
- ritual-site defence equipment.

### Troll equipment

- slab or harness armour;
- massive tools and impact weapons;
- stone or incendiary throwing loads;
- cargo and casualty harnesses;
- portable anchor or portal components; and
- protective fittings for a bonded handler or caster.

Non-casters can still consume arrows, food, armour, and other mundane supplies,
but these should not replace the faction's established match clocks.
Components establish magical capability, caster strain limits repeated magical
action, and anchor capacity limits coordinated mass.

## Progression depth

Giving every goblin the same progression surface as a caster would create more
administration than the already larger human roster and would weaken the
faction's hierarchy. A tiered identity budget is a better candidate:

- **Casters** receive full character depth across spells, authority, anchoring,
  rituals, perception, and strain.
- **Captains, specialists, handlers, and trolls** receive medium depth with a
  few meaningful automatic outcomes.
- **Rank-and-file goblins and orcs** retain persistent identities but use lighter
  traits, scars, and proficiencies.
- **Warbands** can carry collective history and traits such as experienced
  anchor defence, tunnel fighting, disciplined withdrawal, or ritual escort.

All development can remain automatic and policy-influenced. The most memorable
arcane individuals would be the caster circle, renowned captains, unusual
specialists, and surviving trolls, while warband development gives the larger
mass continuity without requiring constant per-person management.

## Questions before acceptance

1. How many casters should a normal force contain, and how many formations can
   one caster lead without making ritual quorum either trivial or unavailable?
2. Which non-caster officers can interpret or modify prior intent, and how is
   succession represented after a caster dies?
3. Does a portable bound standard count as an anchor, an anchor component, or a
   distinct lower-capacity capability?
4. Which named caster and species outcomes remain legible and valuable under
   real-time control?
5. How much individual progression does rank-and-file persistence need before
   warband-level development becomes the clearer representation?
6. Which physical ranged weapons preserve the canonical human advantage in open
   ground?
7. Which equipment belongs to initial content, and which exists only as later
   research, culture, or campaign variation?
8. How do trolls regenerate, what suppresses that regeneration, and when does a
   troll become a damage sponge rather than a tactical body?
9. Do scavenged human electronics create interesting dependency and training
   problems without eroding faction asymmetry?
10. Which proposal elements should become canonical before prototyping, and
    which should wait for observed play?
